§ 01

The problem

Climate decisions get made on a design day and a vendor spreadsheet. The hours that actually bind are invisible.

A 0.4% design dry bulb sizes a coil. It does not tell you how many hours of the year outside air alone would have held the band, how many hours a pad was worth running rather than merely running, or whether your binding constraint is temperature at all. In a Tulsa pad and vent baseline the moisture ceiling binds in 3,851 hours and the temperature margin in 2,952, so the cheapest sensible-cooling upgrade is answering the wrong question.

The hour is the unit of the decision. Whether a strategy is worth capital depends on the joint distribution of dry bulb, moisture and solar across a whole year, and on how that distribution shifts between weather years. One year is an anecdote. In the six-strategy Tulsa example, baseline attainment ranges from a median of 28.9% to a worst year of 27.1%, and the cost ranking of the six strategies is not stable: five years produce two distinct orders.

That instability is the finding. A tool that hid it behind a single ranked list would be more confident and less useful.

§ 02

What it answers

Three site questions
Q1

What does the climate give you free?

Hours where outside air alone holds the crop band, hours where an evaporative pad is thermodynamically worth running, and hours where ventilation can stay at minimum so CO2 enrichment is possible. Tulsa gives 401 free-cooling hours and 244 pad-effective hours. Phoenix, same facility and crop, gives 2,636 and 2,334.

Q2

What is the binding constraint?

Every missed hour is attributed: temperature margin, moisture ceiling, dew-point guardrail, or unmet capacity. Sensible and latent loads are decomposed by source (solar, lights, envelope, infiltration, fans, crop) with the space sensible heat ratio, which is what decides between reheat and a dedicated drying path.

Q3

Which strategy class closes the gap, at what cost and confidence?

Six equipment strategies compared on identical weather, geometry and crop assumptions, across ten weather years, reported as median, worst, spread and trend, with a computed verdict on whether the ranking survives the years and the screened parameter ranges. Class, not brand: there are no performance maps behind this.

§ 03

How it works

Static browser tool, no account
01

Pick a site and the years

Enter a US ZIP, confirm the centroid and time zone, then retrieve NASA POWER hourly weather, use station observations with independent solar, or import a CSV or JSON snapshot. Tulsa 2016 to 2025 is bundled, so you can run ten years offline.

02

Describe the facility and the crop band

Greenhouse, hybrid or indoor. Floor, canopy and envelope areas are separate. Targets are a joint band: day and night temperature, VPD, dew-point guardrail, photoperiod and DLI. Defaults are visibly labeled assumptions, not prescriptions.

03

Add the strategies you are choosing between

Ventilation, evaporative pad, heating, standalone dehumidification, DX with coupled sensible and latent capacity, integrated cooling with reheat, and generic desiccant or hybrid topologies with explicit regeneration energy. Add sensitivity cases on any input.

04

Run, then compare on the common hours

A Web Worker simulates a coupled single-zone balance at one-minute control steps. Comparisons use the common eligible hour set, excluding the warm-up hour of each weather segment. Exports carry the full assumption set, the model version and the pinned PsychroLib commit.

§ 04

What you get

Output, and the basis under it

Multi-year climate risk

Attainment and operating cost as a distribution per strategy: median year, worst year, spread, trend, plus a computed stable or unstable verdict on the cost ranking.

Basis Ten bundled NASA POWER years. Measured example, six strategies over five years: baseline median 28.9%, worst 27.1% in 2025, spread 2.7 pts, ranking not stable (2 orders).

Multi-site comparison

The same facility, crop and strategies across several ZIPs, ranked by free-cooling hours, binding constraint and operating cost.

Basis Measured two-site run, Tulsa 74103 vs Phoenix 85001: 244 vs 2,334 pad-effective hours, 401 vs 2,636 free-cooling hours. Time zone is entered explicitly, the public ZIP catalog has no time-zone column.

Pad viability, not just runtime

Hours and days each component ran, separated from the hours the weather made that component worth running.

Basis Tulsa pad and vent baseline: the pad ran 2,772 h on 296 days, while the weather-side screen rates only 244 h on 65 days as pad-effective.

Outside-air dehumidification screen

Hour by hour, whether ventilating with outside air removes moisture more cheaply than running the machine, on both cost per kg and energy per kg.

Basis Tulsa year against a 2.5 L/kWh dehumidifier: outside air wins on cost per kg in 4,970 h and on energy per kg in 1,752 h. The two counts differ because fan energy and price are different questions.

Sensible and latent, with SHR

Hourly and monthly split of solar, lighting, envelope, infiltration, fan and crop sensible gains against crop and ventilation-import latent load, with the space sensible heat ratio. Losses draw below the axis.

Basis Exposed terms of the same zone balance whose conservation identities close between 1e-16 and 5e-13 relative.

CO2 enrichment window

Hours per strategy where ventilation stays at its minimum, which is when enrichment is physically possible rather than vented away.

Basis The controller's own dispatch record, not a separate estimate. No enrichment feedback on transpiration is modeled, and that is declared in the run assumptions.

Equipment runtime and unmet load

Hours and days each component ran, capacity limits that bound, and hours where demand exceeded capacity, reported rather than absorbed.

Basis Finite-capacity dispatch at one-minute control steps with a staged deadband controller. The older ideal optimizer stays selectable as a labeled upper bound.

Design-basis brief

A printable page for the engineer of record: peak sensible and latent hours with the coincident outdoor state and its frequency, ventilation air requirement, condensate, pad water, free-cooling hours, binding constraint, strategy verdict and evidence tier.

Basis Generated from the run, in the brand's Archive register. It is a basis of design to argue with, not a stamped design.

What the sensitivity screen found

A Morris elementary-effects screen (1,872 full simulations over 104 design points, 8 trajectories, 12 parameters, three weather years, seed 1, reproducible in 290 s) ranks which assumptions move the answer. Crop leaf area and transpiration lead on all three metrics (9.10 pp of attainment), then envelope U value (5.89), then shade fraction (3.43).

The cost ranking is not stable across those ranges: three distinct orders appear, the most common in 61.5% of points. The instability is confined to the DX, hybrid-desiccant and integrated positions, whose median costs sit within 16% of each other. The three cheapest positions are identical in 104 of 104 points. So the screen supports a coarse verdict and refuses a fine one, which is the correct output for this tier.

Morris is a screening method. It gives no probability distribution, no confidence interval and no probability that one strategy beats another. Quantified uncertainty needs calibration against measurements, which is milestone M6.

What this is not

  • Not a calibrated digital twin. No site calibration has been performed.
  • Not benchmarked against an independent greenhouse model. The GreenLight comparison is milestone M5.
  • Not equipment sizing, a tonnage schedule or a stamped mechanical design.
  • Not a manufacturer or brand comparison. No product performance maps are implemented, and generic topologies carry an assumed-performance label.
  • Not a yield, revenue or ROI forecast. Added control hours are not crop value.
  • Not a forecast of any kind. Historical weather years are evidence about a site, not a prediction of the next one.
  • Not a site locator. ZIP centroids are not exact facility locations, and utility candidates are not tariffs.
  • Not spatial. A well-mixed single zone cannot evaluate canopy-to-canopy precision.

Read the caveats with the numbers. Executed checks are in VERIFICATION.md, the independent reviews and open items in AUDIT.md, the sensitivity screen in SENSITIVITY.md.

§ 07

Roadmap

Each rung earns one claim

The route from a coarse screen to a calibrated twin is ordered so the cheap accuracy wins come first: an unconverged controller or a static crop load would make any later benchmark compare artifacts rather than physics. Milestones and acceptance gates are in DIGITAL-TWIN.md.

MilestoneStatusWhat it deliversThe claim it earns
M0 site evaluator Done, v0.2 Multi-year risk, multi-site comparison, sensible and latent split, CO2 window, cost-of-precision sweeps, design-basis brief Results reported as a distribution over ten weather years, not a single year
M1 converged controller Done Staged deadband controller as default, ideal optimizer retained as a labeled upper bound Cost rankings are not artifacts of control cadence (0.38 pp, 0.31% at half the step)
M2 state-coupled crop load Done Stanghellini transpiration from leaf area, absorbed radiation and zone VPD The moisture load responds to the zone state, so dehumidification changes it
M3 structural sensitivity Done Morris screening over 12 parameters and six strategies, with a non-dominated-set stability test Every comparison carries a computed stability label instead of a prose disclaimer
M4 equipment maps Next DX capacity and EIR curves, pad effectiveness against face velocity, wind and stack ventilation, out-of-domain flags Map-based screening where a map was supplied, assumed-constant badge otherwise
M5 benchmarked simulation Planned GreenLight reproduction of the Katzin 2020 case, then a matched Tulsa model-to-model run Tier 3: documented benchmark errors, for the covered conditions only
M6 site calibration Planned Instrumented campaign, identifiable parameter fit, held-out validation report Tier 4: site-calibrated with uncertainty, for the instrumented facility only
